Experimental Analysis of Battery Pack Thermal Management Using Wind Tunnel

Hybrid active-passive cooling system combining forced air convection and paraffin wax PCM for a 16-cell lithium-ion battery pack. Experimental characterization across four wind speeds using a 3D-printed trapezoidal pack with full thermocouple instrumentation.

  • 🔋
    Battery pack 16 cylindrical cells in a 4-row trapezoidal arrangement — wider base facing wind for improved rear-row airflow. 3D printed using Markforged Onyx filament (chopped carbon fiber reinforced).
  • 🌡️
    Thermal simulation — heating papers Heating papers wired in parallel at 9.25 V (two DC power supplies) to simulate battery heat generation. Calibrated to achieve 70°C steady-state — the maximum safe operating temperature.
  • 🧊
    Phase change material Paraffin wax jacketed around each cell as passive thermal buffer. Melts at 40°C, absorbing latent heat and ensuring uniform temperature distribution during the cooling phase.
  • 📡
    Instrumentation — 16 thermocouples + PicoLog DAQ One thermocouple per cell placed at the predicted maximum-temperature location. Connected to PicoLog dataloggers for real-time temperature–time data acquisition and monitoring.
  • 💨
    Wind tunnel — 4 test conditions Tests run at 0, 2, 5, and 10 m/s to characterise cooling effectiveness across the full range from natural convection to high forced convection.

Analysis

Key Findings

  • 2 m/s delivers the biggest relative gain — peak temperature drops from ~82°C to below 60°C with just minimal airflow. PCM latent heat absorption is the primary mechanism at this speed.
  • Safe operation confirmed at 5 m/s and above — peak temps remain under 45°C, well within the 50°C safe limit for Li-ion cells. Cooling speed also improves significantly.
  • Trapezoidal pack geometry validated — the wider inlet face improves airflow penetration to rear rows, reducing the temperature gradient across the pack.
  • Persistent hotspots identified — Row 2 #1, Row 2 #5, and Row 3 #4 consistently showed the highest temperatures across all wind speeds, suggesting local airflow stagnation in those positions.
  • PCM + forced air hybrid is effective — the PCM ensures uniform distribution during low-speed cooling while active airflow handles peak heat rejection, confirming the design hypothesis.
Reflection

Limitations & Future Work

Current limitations

  • Heating papers used instead of real Li-ion cells — actual electrochemical heat generation has transient and non-uniform characteristics not fully captured by constant-power resistive heaters.
  • Only four discrete wind speeds tested — a continuous sweep would better characterise the transition between natural and forced convection regimes.
  • Pure paraffin wax PCM with fixed melt point (40°C) — enhanced PCMs with nano-additives or higher latent heat capacity could extend the passive cooling window.
  • No airflow velocity mapping inside the pack — CFD simulation would clarify the stagnation zones behind the persistent hotspot cells.

Future directions

  • Reposition thermocouples to cell outer surface to measure jacket temperature and validate PCM thermal resistance more directly.
  • Test airflow on the shorter (downstream) base of the trapezoid to quantify its effect on rear-row cell temperatures.
  • Integrate CFD simulation to correlate thermocouple readings with full 3D temperature field in the pack.
