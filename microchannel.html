Microchannel Heat Exchanger Multi-Objective Optimization

High-fidelity CFD modeling of a microchannel cold plate for high-power electronics cooling, combined with NSGA-II genetic algorithm optimization to generate a Pareto-optimal geometry balancing heat dissipation and pressure drop penalty.

Approach

Methodology

  • 01
    High-fidelity CFD modelling Conjugate heat transfer simulations in ANSYS Fluent for a microchannel cold plate targeting ASIC/VRM thermal management on PCIe accelerator cards. Water coolant, 333,333 W/m² heat flux input.
  • 02
    Design of Experiments (CCD) 25-case Central Composite Design varying 4 parameters — channel width W, height H, length L, and Reynolds number Re — to systematically explore the design space and build a statistical performance dataset.
  • 03
    Response Surface Modelling Linear RSM fitted to the DOE results. R² = 0.9318 — model accurately captures how each variable drives heat transfer coefficient h and pressure drop ΔP across the design space.
  • 04
    NSGA-II Multi-Objective Optimisation Weighted-sum NSGA-II sweeping w₁ from 0 to 1 to generate a 6-point Pareto front trading off max heat transfer against min pumping power. Validated against analytical correlations with 13.7% agreement.

Reflection

Limitations & Future Work

Current limitations

  • Linear RSM (R² = 0.9318) may underfit non-linear interactions at extreme parameter combinations — a quadratic or kriging model could improve accuracy in boundary regions.
  • Single-phase water cooling assumed throughout; two-phase or nanofluid coolants could unlock further heat transfer gains beyond what this model captures.
  • Analytical validation at 13.7% agreement — acceptable for design screening, but experimental validation on fabricated samples would be needed before production.
  • Pareto front generated with 6 weighted-sum solutions; a true population-based NSGA-II run would produce a denser, more representative front.

Future directions

  • Extend to manifold microchannel designs to reduce flow non-uniformity across the cold plate area.
  • Incorporate transient thermal response modelling for pulsed load scenarios common in GPU and ASIC workloads.
  • Physical fabrication and bench testing using a high-power test setup to close the simulation-to-hardware loop.
